\noindent\textbf{Background and objective:} Chest radiograph classifiers are often trained and tested on pooled public collections, where internal accuracy can reflect leakage, proxy labels and source style rather than disease. We rebuilt a six-class benchmark (COVID-19, Normal, Pleural Effusion, Pneumonia, Pulmonary Nodule/Mass, Tuberculosis) to measure how far internal metrics overstate generalisation.

\noindent\textbf{Methods:} Images came from official sources with SHA-256 provenance and fixed labelling rules. Splits were grouped by patient where known and by near-duplicates; Montgomery County was held out as an external cohort. An ImageNet-pretrained DenseNet-121 was fine-tuned after Gaussian blur, contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation and low-pass filtering. Test split and external cohort were each evaluated once. A linear probe tested whether image source is recoverable from frozen features.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} Internal test accuracy was \RTestAcc\% (Wilson 95\% CI \RTestAccLo--\RTestAccHi\%; $N=\RNTest$), macro-F1 \RTestMacroFOne\%, macro ROC-AUC \RTestMacroAUC. Externally ($N=\RNExternal$), \RExtSensK/\RExtSensN{} tuberculosis scans were flagged abnormal but only \RExtSpecK/\RExtSpecN{} normal scans were called Normal, and \RExtTBK/\RExtTBN{} were labelled Tuberculosis; most scans were assigned Pneumonia or Nodule/Mass. The probe identified the source with \RProbeImgBalAcc\% balanced accuracy (chance \RProbeImgChance\%). External COVID-19 calls fell from \RRunOneExtCovidCalls{} to \RExtCovidCalls{} when build~2 was used, without better specificity. Our earlier pipeline had patient leakage and external specificity \RVFiveExtSpecK/\RVFiveExtSpecN.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:} In a single training run, internal metrics substantially overstated behaviour on an unseen hospital; class and source were strongly confounded. Leakage-controlled construction, per-source reporting and external sensitivity with specificity should be minimum requirements.
