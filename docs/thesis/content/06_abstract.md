# Abstract

Chest radiography is the most common first-line imaging test for lung disease, but radiologists are scarce in many places. Deep learning models can classify chest X-rays (CXR), yet some published systems report accuracy on test sets that share patients or sources with the training data, so their figures may not describe behaviour at a new hospital. This project builds LungAI, a six-class CXR classifier (COVID-19, Normal, Pleural Effusion, Pneumonia, Pulmonary Nodule / Mass, Tuberculosis) and an auditable, leakage-controlled pipeline around it.

An earlier version (V5) recorded 82.93% internal accuracy. An audit found that 154 of its 1,570 test images (9.8%) shared a patient with the training set, that half of its Pneumonia class consisted of proxy labels, and that on an external cohort it labelled none of 80 normal scans as normal. Its checkpoint is lost. The present work (V6) rebuilds the dataset from the official sources of seven collections, with label rules fixed before training and a split by patient group in which near-duplicates share a group. All leakage checks evaluate to zero, but patient separation is proven only for rows with known patient identity: TBX11K and COVID-19 Radiography rows are grouped per image. The Montgomery County set is the external cohort. It was never used for training or checkpoint selection, but the second V6 run was started after the first run's external results were seen.

The classifier is a DenseNet-121 with ImageNet weights, a global-average-pooling head and a six-way softmax, trained in two phases with one seed (42) on a laptop CPU after a fixed preprocessing chain (blur, CLAHE, low-pass filter, resizing to 224 × 224). It is served by a FastAPI backend (heuristic input gate, Grad-CAM) and a React frontend.
