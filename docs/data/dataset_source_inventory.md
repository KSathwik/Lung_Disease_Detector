## Risks found during this audit

**R1. External cohort probably in training (CRITICAL).**
- *Finding:* Secondary summaries of Rahman et al. (2020) say the public 700 TB images combine NLM Montgomery and Shenzhen (394) with Belarus (306). The publisher's DataPort page lists NLM Montgomery and Shenzhen as sources. V5 has 665 of those 700 images (`Existing_Tuberculosis`, renamed files).
- *Consequence:* if any of Montgomery's 58 TB images are among them, the "external" Montgomery evaluation is contaminated.
- *Why it wasn't caught:* the earlier quarantine checked only names and paths.
- *Status:* UNRESOLVED until `scripts/data/check_external_overlap.py` is run on the two image sets.

**R2. Patient identity unknown for 8,478 V5 images.** Kermany identity is recoverable from filenames, and that recovery revealed 154 leaked test images.

**R3. Label proxies.** These V5 labels are not what their names say:
- *Pneumonia:* 1,395 Pneumonia labels are TBX11K "sick but non-TB" images.
- *Tuberculosis:* 124 TB labels are latent TB.
- *VinDr:* the "single-finding" VinDr images mostly carry several findings.

**R4. Third-party derivatives.** The VinDr and TBX11K images came from Kaggle derivatives, not the publishers. The VinDr images cannot be matched to the official files by checksum.

**R5. Confounding.** COVID-19 comes from one source, Normal and Pneumonia are mostly paediatric (Kermany) or TBX11K, and Effusion is 88% VinDr.

**R6. Undocumented hash method.** The manifest's dHash algorithm is undocumented and does not separate chest X-rays well: 6,650 pairs fall within 2 bits. It cannot support near-duplicate claims.

**R7. Licences unread.** Licences for five sources are UNRESOLVED and must be read before redistribution or publication. No images may be committed to the repository in any case.
